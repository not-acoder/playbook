## Intent

Implement a flexible grading system for formative assessments that uses unlimited resubmissions and generous deadlines to support a growth mindset, ensuring students are not penalized for mistakes made during the initial learning phase.

## Problem

Students enter courses with widely varying levels of prior experience. Traditional grading systems that mark weekly work solely on correctness penalize those who are new to a subject and still learning how to integrate complex concepts. This "snapshot" approach to grading treats every assignment as a final judgment, creating a "deficit thinking" model where early failures can permanently damage a student's grade and motivation. This is particularly problematic for foundational courses where students need time to experiment and learn from errors without the fear of immediate, irredeemable penalties.

## Solution

Shift formative assessments—such as weekly labs, coding exercises, or knowledge checks—from evaluative endpoints to iterative learning opportunities. This is achieved through two primary mechanisms: flexible deadlines and multiple submission attempts. Students are encouraged to submit work by an initial "target" date but are allowed to redo and resubmit assignments as many times as needed until they achieve mastery. By decoupling the learning process from grade penalties, this system allows students to focus on identifying and closing their own knowledge gaps, ultimately increasing their engagement and improving their performance on subsequent summative assessments.

## Applicability

This play is highly effective in large introductory courses, such as CS0 or CS1, and is well-suited for both on-campus and online environments. It works best for assignments that allow for revision, such as programming projects or short-answer responses. A significant tradeoff is the potential for student procrastination due to the lack of strict weekly deadlines. Additionally, managing unlimited resubmissions in large classes requires efficient feedback loops, often necessitating the use of autograding tools or interactive textbooks to maintain consistent evaluation standards without overwhelming teaching staff.

## How to Implement

Start by identifying all formative assessments in the course and clearly labeling them as "learning opportunities." Establish a system where these assignments remain open for resubmission throughout the term (or until a broad end-of-unit deadline). When grading, use a coarse proficiency scale (e.g., a 0–4 scale) rather than a percentage-based one to simplify feedback. To manage the workload in large classes, utilize autograders that provide immediate feedback on student code or quiz responses. Frequently remind students that their early struggles are expected and that they have the power to "repair" their grade by persisting until they meet the mastery criteria. Finally, track student resubmission rates to identify those who may need additional support to stay on track despite the flexible schedule.
