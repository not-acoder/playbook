## Intent

Use a token-based system to manage assignment resubmissions, providing students with limited opportunities to apply feedback and demonstrate mastery without overwhelming instructor grading capacity.

## Problem

Traditional grading systems often use partial credit as a proxy for progress, which can inadvertently discourage students from deeply engaging with instructor feedback. When a student receives a "passing" but mediocre grade, they may lack the motivation to correct their mistakes, leading to a cumulative gap in knowledge that hinders success on future, more complex assignments. However, allowing unlimited resubmissions can lead to unsustainable workloads for instructors and encourage students to procrastinate or submit low-quality work repeatedly in the hope of "guessing" the correct answer.

## Solution

Implement a structured resubmission policy that replaces partial credit with a binary "pass/fail" or "meets/not yet" marking scheme. To balance student growth with instructor capacity, use a "token" system to cap the total number of resubmissions allowed per student. This encourages students to be selective and purposeful in their revisions, ensuring they treat every "at-bat" seriously. By coupling high-quality feedback with a clear path to grade repair, instructors can incentivize students to internalize course concepts and demonstrate genuine mastery before moving on to advanced material.

## Applicability

This play is ideal for skill-based courses where mastery of foundational concepts is essential for future success, such as mathematics, computer science, or foreign languages. It works best for formative assignments like labs, problem sets, or short writing tasks. A primary tradeoff is the administrative need to track token usage and manage the logistics of re-grading. It is particularly effective in courses where automated grading tools can handle the initial pass/fail check, allowing instructors to focus their feedback on more complex, non-functional requirements like code style or logical elegance.

## How to Implement

Start by designating specific "core" assignments as eligible for resubmission. Establish a clear "mastery" threshold (e.g., 85% or all functional requirements met) that must be reached to earn credit. At the start of the term, grant each student a fixed number of tokens (e.g., four per semester) or use a time-based system where tokens expire weekly. When a student receives a "Not Yet Proficient" mark, they must use a token to resubmit their revised work within a set timeframe (e.g., one week after receiving feedback). Use your LMS to track token balances and provide a transparent record of which assignments have been successfully mastered. Encourage students to save tokens for truly challenging units, fostering a sense of strategic ownership over their own learning process.
