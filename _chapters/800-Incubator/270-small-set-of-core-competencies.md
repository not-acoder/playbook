## Intent

Focus student effort on a limited set of essential skills (6–8 per course) to simplify assessment, increase focus, and provide space for multiple attempts without increasing instructor workload.

## Problem

Instructors often over-specify learning outcomes into fine-grained, specific tasks that quickly become unwieldy for both students and faculty. Students face cognitive overload trying to track dozens of micro-outcomes, while instructors find it administratively impossible to offer the necessary revisions or reassessments for a high-volume list of objectives. This lack of focus often results in students prioritizing points over deep mastery of core concepts.

## Solution

Distill the course curriculum into a small, clearly defined set of 6–8 core competencies that students must demonstrate to succeed. These competencies are not just a list of topics, but a set of critical skills and knowledge integral to the course's overall success. For example, a Computer Science course might identify 'Complete induction' or 'Establishing topological compactness' as specific competencies. By limiting the number, the instructor can offer multiple assessment methods (interviews, quizzes, reports) and allow for multiple attempts on each competency throughout the term.

## Applicability

This play works well in courses with clearly definable and demonstrable skills, such as introductory and upper-level STEM courses (e.g., Computer Science, Mathematics). It is especially effective when combined with a hybrid assessment system that includes interviews, online quizzes, and written reports. Constraints include the initial time required for the instructor to map the course content to a smaller set of competencies. Tradeoffs involve shifting from many low-stakes points to fewer, higher-stakes (but repeatable) competency assessments, which may initially cause student anxiety about 'all-or-nothing' grading if not accompanied by a policy of multiple attempts.

## How to Implement

To implement this play, follow these steps: (1) List all current learning outcomes, concepts, and skills covered in the course. (2) Identify the 'critical path' of skills and knowledge that are fundamental for students to move to the next course in the sequence. (3) Group similar or redundant outcomes together, collapsing them into 6–8 broad but measurable categories. (4) For each category, define what 'Mastery' and 'Proficiency' look like on a five-point scale (Mastery, Approaching Mastery, Proficiency, Basic, Inconclusive). (5) Introduce a 'Welcome Interview' competency as the first task to ensure students understand the course policies and the new grading structure. (6) Provide students with the full list of competencies and the corresponding grading rubrics at the start of the semester to ensure transparency.
