## Intent

Replace ambiguous numerical points with a five-level descriptive scale (Mastery to Inconclusive) to provide clear, actionable feedback and a more accurate measure of student proficiency.

## Problem

Traditional points-based grading is mathematically flawed and cognitively confusing. A score like '8/10' is ambiguous—it doesn't tell the student whether they missed 20% of the content or made minor errors on 100% of it. Furthermore, averaging points across assignments obscures actual progress; a student who fails early and masters later may have the same average as one who stays mediocre throughout, even though their final proficiency levels are vastly different.

## Solution

Employ a standardized five-point scale for every core competency in the course. Each level (Mastery, Approaching Mastery, Proficiency, Basic, Inconclusive) is tied to a specific qualitative description of student performance. Instead of earning points that are averaged, students work to achieve specific competency levels across 6–8 core objectives. Final course grades are then determined by a transparent mapping of achieved competency levels (e.g., an 'A' requires four 'Mastery' ratings and the rest at 'Approaching Mastery') rather than a mathematical mean.

## Applicability

This scale is highly effective in STEM and professional programs where specific skills must be demonstrated before advancing. It works best in courses where competencies can be assessed multiple times through varied formats (interviews, quizzes, code reviews). Constraints include the need for a clear mapping system from competency levels to final letter grades. Tradeoffs involve moving away from the 'familiar' 100-point scale, which may require initial effort to explain to students and academic advisors.

## How to Implement

To implement the five-point scale, follow these steps: (1) Define 6–8 core competencies for your course. (2) Adopt the following standardized labels and criteria: (a) Mastery (A): Complete, detailed understanding; ability to apply material in novel situations. (b) Approaching Mastery (B): Considerable understanding; can apply material to scenarios not explicitly covered. (c) Proficiency (C): Understands all material at the expected undergraduate level (e.g., knows all definitions and standard applications). (d) Basic (D): Limited or incomplete understanding; not yet adequate for the next course level. (e) Inconclusive (F): Performance does not allow the instructor to determine ability, or work was not submitted. (3) Create a 'mapping table' for final grades. Example: 'A' = 4 Mastery + 4 Approaching Mastery; 'B' = 2 Mastery + 2 Proficiency + 4 Approaching Mastery; 'C' = All at Proficiency or higher. (4) Configure your LMS to use these labels as the primary grading method, hiding 'under-the-hood' points if necessary. (5) Provide students with specific feedback and advice on how to move from their current level to the next (e.g., 'To move from Proficiency to Approaching Mastery, try applying this logic to the unassigned challenge problem').
