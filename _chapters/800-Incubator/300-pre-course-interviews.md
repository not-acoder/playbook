## Intent

Use 1-1 interviews at the term's start to identify prerequisite knowledge gaps and build student-instructor rapport, especially in skill-sequenced disciplines like STEM.

## Problem

Instructors often assume students entering a course have mastered the foundational concepts of its prerequisites. However, traditional points-based grading often obscures a student's true level of competency, as they may have 'learned for the test' or forgotten concepts over time. Without an early diagnostic, instructors may pitch their material at an inappropriate level, leaving some students behind from the first week.

## Solution

Schedule short (typically 15–20 minute) 1-1 oral interviews with every student during the first two weeks of the term. These interviews serve as a diagnostic tool for prerequisite knowledge and an 'icebreaker' for the course's grading policies. Instead of a formal test, the interview is an open-ended dialogue where the instructor can probe for deeper understanding, providing immediate feedback and support to students who may be starting with gaps in their knowledge.

## Applicability

This play is essential for sequenced courses where mastery of 'CS1' or 'Algebra' is critical for success in 'CS2' or 'Calculus'. It is also highly effective for neurodiverse learners and English as a Second Language (ESL) students, as it allows the instructor to adjust the language and delivery of questions in real-time. The primary constraint is the significant upfront time commitment for the instructor. The tradeoff is a reduction in early-semester failures and a stronger, more transparent learning relationship with students.

## How to Implement

To implement pre-course interviews, follow these steps: (1) Set aside several days at the beginning of the semester for 'Evaluation Days' with no new content delivered. (2) Use an online sign-up sheet to schedule 15–20 minute slots for every student. (3) Prepare a set of open-ended, conceptual questions rather than simple definitional ones (e.g., 'What would you consider when choosing between a dynamic array and a linked list?' rather than 'What is an array?'). (4) During the interview: (a) Provide laminated printouts of the questions to accommodate different processing styles. (b) Offer scratch paper or a whiteboard for students to sketch diagrams or logic. (c) Start with 'icebreaker' questions about course procedures to reduce anxiety. (d) Be prepared to pivot to simpler, definitional questions if a student is clearly struggling, using the interview as a teaching moment to correct misconceptions immediately. (5) Use the findings to calibrate the first few weeks of instruction, focusing extra attention on common prerequisite gaps identified across the cohort.
