## Intent

Weight formative and summative assessments to reflect student growth and mastery, using formative work to establish a passing baseline and summative work to determine the final grade within the passing range.

## Problem

Traditional 100-point scales are heavily biased toward failure, providing 60 points for a failing grade and only 41 points for success. This structure penalizes students for early mistakes and discourages persistence, especially for those from diverse backgrounds who may start with fewer resources or less prior knowledge. Standard systems often fail to distinguish between the learning process (formative) and the demonstration of final mastery (summative), leading to grades that reflect a student's initial struggles rather than their ultimate proficiency.

## Solution

Divide course assessments into two distinct categories: formative and summative. Formative assessments (e.g., interactive readings, coding labs, weekly quizzes) should be designed to support learning and allow for multiple attempts and flexible deadlines without penalty. Weighting these formative activities at 60–70% of the final grade ensures that any student who demonstrates hard work and perseverance can achieve a passing grade. Summative assessments (e.g., midterm and final exams, major projects) should then comprise the remaining 30–40%, serving as the measure of individual mastery that determines the specific grade level (A, B, C) within the passing category.

## Applicability

This play is highly effective in large introductory courses and online environments where students have diverse life schedules and prior experience. It is particularly useful when instructors must report grades on a 100-point scale but want to implement equitable practices. A primary tradeoff is the potential for student procrastination due to flexible deadlines; however, evidence suggests that the ability to resubmit work actually increases engagement and improves outcomes in subsequent courses. Instructors should also consider the administrative effort of managing multiple submissions, which may require autograding tools or structured feedback loops.

## How to Implement

Start by categorizing all current assignments into formative (practice-based) and summative (outcome-based) buckets. Set the final grade weighting to at least 60% for formative and no more than 40% for summative. Ensure that formative tasks allow for redos—ideally until mastery is achieved—and provide flexible submission windows to accommodate different learning paces. To manage the workload, utilize autograders for formative tasks if possible. Clearly communicate to students that their early "failures" on formative work are expected parts of the learning process and will not prevent them from achieving a high final grade if they continue to persevere and demonstrate mastery on the summative checkpoints.
