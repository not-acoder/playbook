title: Credit for Completion, not Quality
slug: credit-for-completion
---

## Intent

Foster a low-stakes learning environment and encourage consistent effort by awarding full credit for the completion of practice activities, regardless of initial correctness.

## Problem

Traditional A-F grading on every assignment often punishes students for the 'trial and error' necessary for learning, especially in high-anxiety subjects like statistics. This performance pressure can cause students to avoid challenging problems, procrastinate, or focus on external success markers rather than meaningful engagement and long-term retention of the material.

## Solution

This practice divorces 'practice' from 'performance' by awarding full points for the completion of formative tasks such as problem sets, take-home quizzes, or observation reports. The instructor reviews correct answers collectively in the next session, providing immediate feedback without the penalty of a low grade. This shifts the student's focus from 'getting it right for the grade' to 'engaging with the material to learn,' while still providing the accountability of a completion score.

## Applicability

This play is highly effective in introductory or 'gateway' courses where student anxiety is high and foundational skill-building is the primary goal. It is less suitable for advanced courses where precision and quality of performance are the primary learning objectives. Tradeoffs include the risk of 'low-effort' completion by a small percentage of students (typically <10%) and the need for instructors to provide robust collective feedback to ensure students don't reinforce incorrect assumptions.

## How to Implement

1. Identify high-value practice activities (e.g., daily problem sets, weekly take-home quizzes) that are essential for skill development. 2. Explicitly state in the syllabus that these activities will be graded on completion only. 3. Require students to submit their work or bring it to class for small-group discussion. 4. During class, provide the correct answers and discuss the underlying concepts, allowing students to self-correct their work. 5. Award full credit to any student who submitted a complete attempt. 6. Use high-stakes summative exams (graded on quality) to verify that the practice led to actual mastery, ensuring the completion-based credit supports rather than replaces performance standards.
