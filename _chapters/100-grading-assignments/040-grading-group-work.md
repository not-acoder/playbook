## Intent

Provide a more fair and mathematically accurate assessment for individuals on group projects by assessing each member's learning individually after completion, rather than assigning a single grade to the entire group.

## Problem

Group work is intended to facilitate learning through collaboration, yet traditional assessment often uses a single group grade. This approach obscures individual achievement, as it fails to reflect what each student uniquely learned and hides uneven contributions. Furthermore, group grades often penalize high-performing students who may feel resentment or frustration when bearing the burden of lower-achieving peers, while also failing to provide a clear indicator of mastery for any individual. Strategies used to "correct" group grades often mistakenly measure student behavior rather than actual content mastery.

## Solution

To ensure mathematical accuracy and fairness, instructors must shift the focus from the group product to individual learning outcomes. The purpose of group work should be clearly framed as a mechanism for students to learn specific skills and content together, not merely to produce a final artifact. Instructors should establish clear norms for collaboration and provide frequent feedback, but ultimately evaluate each student individually after the project is complete. This maintains individual accountability and ensures that each student is motivated to actively engage and master the material, knowing their own grade reflects their unique understanding.

## Applicability

This play is highly flexible and works across various course sizes and disciplines where collaborative projects are used. It is particularly effective in reducing student resentment and increasing individual engagement in group settings. However, instructors should consider the tradeoff of increased grading time, as assessing each student individually requires more effort than assigning a single group grade. It works best when individual assessment criteria are transparent from the start, ensuring students understand they are accountable for their own mastery of the group's work.

## How to Implement

Instructors can choose from several strategies to assess individual learning post-collaboration. For large classes, traditional exams with questions tailored to the group project's concepts can efficiently gauge individual mastery. In smaller settings, oral exams or short interviews allow instructors to ask follow-up questions, making it a powerful tool for probing depth of understanding. Project extensions offer another path, where each student performs a further refinement or builds a new component upon the group's work to demonstrate their independent skill gains. Alternatively, requiring individual annotations on the group product allows students to explain the underlying logic and context of specific sections, providing clear evidence of their personal contribution and grasp of the material.
