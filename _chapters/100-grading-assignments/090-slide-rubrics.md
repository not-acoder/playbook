## Intent

Grade students based on the individual progress they make across rubric levels throughout a course, rather than assigning grades solely based on their final performance level relative to a fixed benchmark.

## Problem

Standard rubrics often evaluate students against a static, one-size-fits-all expectation of competency. This model fails to recognize or reward the significant growth made by students who start at a lower proficiency level, while allowing high-performing students to "coast" without being challenged to improve. Consequently, struggling students may feel despondent and demotivated by consistent low grades despite their hard work, while advanced students lack the incentive to further refine their skills.

## Solution

Implement a 'slide rubric' system where each student is assigned an individualized target level based on their starting proficiency. The instructor first assesses a student's initial skill level through a pretest or early assignment. Subsequent work is then graded based on the student's movement relative to their specific baseline. For example, a student starting at a "Developing" level who reaches "Proficient" may earn an A for their growth, while a student who starts at "Advanced" but only delivers "Proficient" work may earn a lower grade for failing to maintain or exceed their established standard. This ensures that every student, regardless of their starting point, is motivated to push themselves toward their own next level of excellence.

## Applicability

This play is ideal for courses with multiple, iterative assessments of the same skills, such as writing-intensive classes or foundational math. It works best when the primary goal is to foster a growth mindset and reward persistence. A significant tradeoff is the administrative complexity of tracking individual baselines and explaining the individualized grading logic to students, who may be accustomed to traditional comparative grading. It also requires a reliable pre-assessment to set fair initial placements and prevent students from "gaming" the system by underperforming early on.

## How to Implement

Begin the course with a comprehensive pretest to establish an accurate "initial position" for every student on a multi-level rubric (e.g., a 1–9 scale). Assign each student a starting target level based on this data. For subsequent assignments, calibrate the grade cutoffs based on these targets: if a student's target is Level 3, a rubric score of 4 earns a B, while a score of 5 earns an A. To ensure continued challenge, adjust a student's target upward by one or two levels once they consistently exceed their current position. Maintain transparency by having brief instructional conversations with students about their placement and the specific goals they need to achieve to "slide" up the rubric and earn their desired grade.
