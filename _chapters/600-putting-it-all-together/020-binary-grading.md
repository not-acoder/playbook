## Intent

Use a binary pass/fail grading model combined with unlimited retakes and a simplified count-based calculation to remove the stress of point accumulation and shift student focus toward genuine mastery.

## Problem

Traditional points-based grading is often the most contentious aspect of a course, leading to frequent disputes over partial credit and distracting students from learning feedback. Learners often interpret low numerical marks as personal failures rather than necessary steps in the learning process, fostering a "grade-grubbing" culture where the goal is to earn a score rather than to master the material. Furthermore, fixed deadlines can penalize students who encounter life challenges or require more time to grasp complex concepts, leading to cumulative knowledge gaps.

## Solution

Implement a binary grading system where every assignment is evaluated as either "Satisfactory" or "Unsatisfactory." To support this model, provide students with unlimited opportunities to revise and resubmit their work based on qualitative feedback until they reach the required proficiency. Course grades are then determined by a simple count of mastered activities rather than a weighted average of scores. This approach is supplemented by recommending a clear instructional pace through "soft" deadlines and using mixed assessment types—such as in-class quizzes, take-home projects, and individual explorations—to allow students multiple pathways to demonstrate their competency.

## Applicability

This play is highly effective in upper-level undergraduate courses with small-to-medium enrollments (25–40 students) where deep engagement with complex material is required. It is particularly valuable for fostering a supportive, non-adversarial relationship between instructor and student. However, a significant tradeoff is the potential for student procrastination due to flexible deadlines, which may require the instructor to provide structured milestones. Additionally, the workload for instructors and TAs can increase significantly due to the volume of resubmissions, making it essential to streamline the re-grading process or use common retake sessions.

## How to Implement

Start by defining a set of core activities and determining how many "Satisfactory" marks are required for each letter grade (e.g., 6 for a C, 12 for an A). Grade all work on a binary scale, providing detailed comments rather than points; for exams, you can use a points-based intermediate score where 50% earns one activity credit and 85% earns two. Establish a policy of unlimited retakes for any unsatisfactory work up until the final week of the term. To manage the grading load, schedule specific "common retake blocks" each week where students can come to a central location to re-attempt quizzes. Use your LMS to track the total count of completed activities and provide students with a "recommended pace" calendar to help them avoid a late-semester backlog of work.
