## Intent

Students commit to a transparent contract specifying tasks and behaviors needed for particular course grades, while a built-in safety net ensures that no student is penalized relative to their conventional calculated grade.

## Problem

Students commonly experience traditional grading in operations and similar STEM courses as opaque and high stakes, with limited control over how their effort translates into final grades. Instructors who experiment with contract grading worry that the system may inadvertently harm some students or inflate grades without evidence.

## Solution

The courses implement grading contracts under which students agree to complete specified sets of assignments and meet participation or quality targets for a chosen course grade, while the instructor retains a parallel conventional points-based calculation. At the end of the course, the instructor computes both the contract-based grade and the traditional calculated grade and awards whichever is higher, ensuring that the contract cannot lower a student's grade.

## Applicability

Works well in undergraduate STEM-related courses with multiple graded components over the term (homework, projects, exams, participation). Effective in contexts where instructors want to trial contract grading but must reassure stakeholders that no student will be disadvantaged.

Tradeoffs/limitations: Maintaining both contract tracking and traditional calculations increases instructor workload. Some students may underutilize the contract if they rely on the safety net and ignore contract requirements.

Not a good fit when: Grading structures are very simple (e.g., single exam) and do not support multiple contract conditions. Institutional systems cannot accommodate nuanced grade adjustments or dual calculation schemes.

## How to Implement

1. Define clearly what constitutes completion for each major assignment or activity (e.g., number of tasks submitted, deadlines met, minimum quality levels). Bundle these requirements into contract options that correspond to target grades (e.g., B-level contract, A-level contract).
2. Early in the term, present the contract options and ask students to select and sign the contract that matches their desired grade and anticipated workload. Record each student's contract choice and maintain a simple tracking log throughout the term.
3. Keep a conventional points-based gradebook (or weighted average) as if the course were graded traditionally. Do not use the contract to reduce standards on individual assessments; instead, use it to structure which assignments and behaviors matter for a given grade.
4. At term's end, determine the contract-based grade for each student (based on contract completion) and the traditional calculated grade. Award the higher of the two as the final grade, documenting how often contracts change outcomes for future evaluation.

## Examples

Across three semesters in two undergraduate operations courses, the contract had a positive impact on the final grade for 43% and 57% of students, respectively.

Most contract-related improvements were by one grade step (e.g., B to B+), though a few students gained four or five grade steps (e.g., from D+ to B+).

In a smaller graduate course, 6 of 10 students earned a higher grade under the contract than under pure calculation, 1 saw no change, and 3 would have been lower under the contract but instead kept the higher calculated grade due to the safety net.
