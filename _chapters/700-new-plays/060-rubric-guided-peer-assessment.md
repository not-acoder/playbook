## Intent

Students use a holistic peer-assessment rubric to evaluate simulation or performance tasks, with peer scores contributing meaningfully to grades while maintaining reliability and clear criteria.

## Problem

High-stakes performance assessments in STEM (e.g., labs, simulations, project demos) often rely entirely on instructor judgment, which can introduce bias, bottleneck feedback, and limit student engagement with assessment criteria. Without structured tools, peer assessment can be inconsistent and unfair, limiting its usefulness in grading.

## Solution

The course implements a rubric-guided peer assessment system in which students rate each other's simulation performances using a validated holistic rubric with clearly defined items and a continuous rating scale. Peer scores are used for grading and feedback, and the rubric's design (item selection, scoring scale, and number of raters) is optimized to achieve acceptable reliability in high-stakes contexts.

## Applicability

Works well in simulation-based or lab-based courses where students perform complex procedures or demonstrations (e.g., medical simulations, engineering design reviews, lab practicals). Effective in settings where many performances must be evaluated in a short time, making sole instructor grading impractical.

Tradeoffs/limitations: Requires careful rubric design and pilot testing to ensure validity and reliability. Students need orientation to use the rubric appropriately and avoid superficial rating.

Not a good fit when: Performances are highly confidential or sensitive, limiting peer observation. Class size or scheduling prevents sufficient peer raters per performance.

## How to Implement

1. Identify critical components of the performance (e.g., procedural sequence, interpretation accuracy, communication). Create a holistic rubric with one item per key component, each describing performance expectations at different quality levels.
2. Use a continuous rating scale (e.g., 10, 8, 6, 4, 2, 0) for each item to allow nuanced evaluations while keeping scoring simple. Keep the assessment form concise enough for peers to complete during or immediately after performances.
3. Ensure each student's performance is rated by multiple peers to increase score reliability. Determine what portion of the performance grade will come from peer scores versus instructor scores, using the same rubric where possible.
4. Before graded use, give students practice with the rubric on sample performances and discuss scoring decisions to calibrate expectations.

## Examples

The developed holistic rubric contained 10 items, each with a maximum score of 10 points, covering elements such as sequential interpretation of EKG, accurate EKG interpretation, diagnosis, ACLS algorithm sequence, scenario management, pharmaceutical treatment, pharmacological mechanism, interpersonal skills, communication skills, and learning responsibility.

Rubric items were rated on a 10–8–6–4–2–0 scale, yielding a continuous rating scheme that supported reliable peer grading.

Validity and reliability analyses indicated that, with this rubric and an appropriate number of peer raters, peer assessment scores were sufficiently reliable for use in simulation grading.
