## Intent

Students receive quality feedback from multiple sources and engage in regular reflection to assess their own progress, enabling bidirectional dialogue between educators and students.

## Problem

Learning occurs when students change their thinking or behavior. Traditional grading systems that emphasize points may obfuscate the feedback necessary for this learning to occur. When students receive a grade without meaningful feedback, they know where they stand in a hierarchy but not how to improve or what specifically they learned.

Furthermore, traditional grading creates a unidirectional power dynamic where instructors judge students. This prevents the collaborative relationship needed for students to become co-creators of knowledge rather than passive recipients of instruction.

## Solution

Incorporate various methods for students to receive quality feedback and multiple opportunities for students to reflect on and evaluate their own learning. Together, these approaches enable bidirectional dialogue between educators and students, supporting students in becoming co-creators of knowledge.

**Quality Feedback:** Feedback that is process-oriented, personal, informal, and genuine establishes trustworthy communication. Feedback can flow in multiple directions - from instructor to student, student to instructor, peer to peer, and from the whole class.

**Reflection:** Regular self-evaluation assignments require students to assess their own progress, identify areas for growth, and articulate what they've learned. This metacognitive practice helps students develop agency in their learning process.

## Applicability

This approach increases pedagogical effectiveness of any grading practice, but it's especially beneficial in equitable grading practices that avoid penalizing students during the learning process.

**When this is most effective:**
- Courses using ungrading, specifications grading, or mastery-based approaches
- Contexts where developing metacognitive skills is a learning goal
- Settings where instructor has capacity to provide individualized feedback
- Courses prioritizing revision and improvement over single-attempt assessment

**Resource considerations:**
- Providing quality feedback requires significant instructor time, especially in large classes
- Multiple rounds of reflection throughout the semester require planning and integration into course structure
- Technology tools (discussion boards, feedback apps, peer review platforms) can help manage workload

## How to Implement

**1. Establish multiple feedback channels**

Create diverse avenues for feedback throughout the course:

**Instructor to student:** Provide written comments on assignments focused on process and improvement rather than justification of points. Make feedback personal by referencing specific student work and genuine by offering authentic reactions.

**Student to instructor:** Integrate anonymous feedback mechanisms so students can voice concerns, suggest improvements to the course, or ask clarifying questions. Model for students that even educators learn and improve over time.

**Peer feedback:** Structure peer review activities where students provide feedback to classmates. Provide guidelines or rubrics to focus peer feedback on substantive issues.

**Whole class feedback:** Use class discussions to provide collective feedback on common challenges or exemplary work (anonymized).

**2. Design a reflection schedule**

Plan 5-6 self-evaluation assignments throughout the semester, varying in depth and focus:

**Cursory reflections** (2-3 times): Brief check-ins asking students to assess where they are, what's working, and what they're struggling with.

**Intensive reflections** (2-3 times): Deeper self-evaluations requiring students to reflect on:
- Progress in relation to course learning outcomes
- Attendance and participation quality
- Willingness to accept and use feedback
- Risks taken in their learning
- Their own learning process and strategies
- Pride felt concerning specific learning achievements

The source author notes these assignments provide insight into students' working processes, struggles, and triumphs that wouldn't emerge through traditional grading.

**3. Structure effective reflection prompts**

Design prompts that encourage meaningful self-assessment:

Sample prompts from the source and related literature:
- "What progress have you made toward [specific learning outcome]? What evidence demonstrates this progress?"
- "Describe a moment when you accepted feedback and used it to improve your work. What changed as a result?"
- "What risk did you take in your learning this week? What did you learn from it?"
- "Compare your work from early in the semester to now. What growth do you notice?"
- "What are you most proud of learning? Why does this learning matter to you?"

**4. Provide formative feedback regularly**

Offer feedback at multiple points during the learning process, not just on final products:

- Comment on drafts before final submissions
- Provide verbal feedback during class discussions
- Offer written feedback on reflection assignments themselves
- Hold brief check-in meetings with students to discuss their progress

Focus feedback on process ("I notice you're developing stronger analysis skills by connecting multiple sources") rather than just outcomes ("This earned 85%").

**5. Create opportunities for feedback dialogue**

Rather than one-way communication, establish genuine dialogue:

- Ask students to respond to your feedback, explaining how they'll use it
- Invite students to request specific types of feedback they need
- Host office hours focused on discussing feedback and next steps
- Include peer feedback discussions where students talk through feedback they received

**6. Close the feedback loop**

Help students see that feedback leads to improvement:

- Require students to explain how they incorporated previous feedback in revised work
- Ask students to track their progress on specific skills across multiple assignments
- Facilitate conversations where students identify patterns in feedback they're receiving
- Celebrate when students demonstrate growth based on earlier feedback
